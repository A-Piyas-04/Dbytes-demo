# Doshomik Bytes — Research Features & Monetization Proposal

**Status:** Proposal for client discussion — features, limits, and prices are not final.

## 1. Goal

Make AI/ML research papers easier for students and enthusiasts to discover, understand, and discuss, especially in **Bangla and English**.

**Our principle:** Keep essential learning free. Offer optional Premium access to higher-cost AI features so the app can cover its own operating expenses without prioritizing profit.

The existing app structure stays: **Reels · Learn · Practice · Research · Profile**.

## 2. Proposed Research Features

| Feature | What the student can do | Where the information comes from |
|---|---|---|
| **1. Discover Papers** | Browse and search interesting AI/ML research papers, including trending papers. | Paper details from **OpenAlex**, **Semantic Scholar**, and **Hugging Face**; saved in our database. |
| **2. Paper Card** | See the paper's question, main idea, findings, limitations, difficulty, and a short glossary in simple English/Bangla. | Original paper details + **AI-generated explanation created once per paper**, checked where possible, and stored in our database. |
| **3. Figure-First Reader** | Explore important graphs and diagrams one by one. Try a quick question before revealing the explanation. | Figures from original papers **only when their licenses allow reuse**; explanations prepared with AI and saved. Otherwise, link to the original paper. |
| **4. Three-Step Reading Tracker** | Mark a paper as (1) explored, (2) studied, or (3) reproduced/implemented. Keep notes and see progress. | User actions, notes, and progress stored in our database. No AI needed. |
| **5. Learn Before Reading** | See prerequisite topics for a difficult paper; open related Learn lessons, reels, or practice questions, then return to the paper. | Prerequisites suggested by AI when preparing the Paper Card; matched to **our own Learn content**. |
| **6. Related Papers & Reading Paths** | See which papers came before or after another paper; follow a simple recommended reading order. | Citation and related-paper data from **Semantic Scholar/OpenAlex**; curated paths from creators. |
| **7. Reviews & Discussions** | Read available expert criticisms of a paper and discuss ideas with other learners. | Publicly available reviews from **OpenReview** (where available); comments created by app users. |
| **8. Paper Clubs (later)** | Join a creator-led group studying the same paper together. | Existing paper content + club schedules and discussions saved in our database. |
| **9. Research Landscape Map (later, premium-enabled)** | Choose a topic (e.g., "small language models"). Get a **structured map** of main approaches, representative papers, how they connect, and open questions worth investigating. Not a generic citation network. | OpenAlex/Semantic Scholar retrieve a capped set of papers and citation relationships; AI groups them into themes and explains links; the map is saved for reuse. |
| **10. Paper Comparison Matrix (later, premium-enabled)** | Select up to 5 papers and receive a side-by-side table of **methods, datasets, reported metrics, strengths, weaknesses, and key differences**, with source references. | Metadata and accessible paper passages + reported results; AI extracts comparable fields. Missing or incomparable results are marked clearly; output is saved. |
| **11. Research Claim & Evidence Check (later, premium-enabled)** | Pick a paper's important claim and see **which experiment supports it, which assumptions it depends on, and what the evidence does not establish**. | Extracted paper sections/figures when permitted, reviews from OpenReview when available, and citation context; AI creates a traceable claim-to-evidence breakdown. This is not automatic fact verification. |
| **12. Reproduction Roadmap (later, premium-enabled)** | Turn a paper into a practical **implementation checklist**: prerequisites, code/data availability, experiment steps, evaluation plan, and likely blockers. | Paper's methods and references, linked repositories and datasets when available; AI drafts an actionable plan; it **does not run experiments** or promise reproducibility. |

**Important difference:** Features 1–8 are the free learning foundation. Features 9–12 are structured, on-demand research workflows. They may require multiple source lookups and AI processing, so each run has a bounded scope and a usage limit. They are **proposals**, not guaranteed research findings.

## 3. Simple User Flow

1. **Discover:** A student opens **Research** and chooses a paper.
2. **Understand:** They read the **Paper Card** in Bangla or English.
3. **Prepare:** If the paper is too difficult, the app suggests lessons in **Learn**.
4. **Read actively:** They explore figures, answer small questions, and follow the three reading steps.
5. **Go deeper:** They look at related papers, expert reviews, and community discussion.
6. **Track progress:** Their reading history appears in **Profile**.
7. **Optional advanced workflow:** They create a topic landscape, compare selected papers, examine the evidence behind a claim, or build a reproduction plan. Each produces a saved, structured output—not a chat conversation.

## 4. How the Data and AI Will Work

**Simple flow:**

**Research sources → Our backend → Our database → Research tab**

- **Research sources:** OpenAlex, Semantic Scholar, Hugging Face, and OpenReview provide paper details, relationships, trends, and available reviews. arXiv/original publishers remain the source for reading full papers.
- **Database:** We propose **PostgreSQL** to store papers, prepared explanations, prerequisites, reading progress, and discussions. This is a proposed choice, not a claim about the existing app's database.
- **AI:** A paid model (for example, Gemini) prepares simple explanations, bilingual text, and figure explanations **once for each paper**, not once for every student.
- **App:** Students normally receive information from our database, so opening the same Paper Card repeatedly does **not** generate new AI bills.
- **On-demand AI:** Landscape maps, comparison matrices, claim checks, and reproduction roadmaps may make paid model calls. Each job uses a limited number of papers/pages, and the results are saved for reuse. The API key stays on our server, not in the mobile app.

**Example:** If 5,000 students open one Paper Card, they see the same stored explanation. We do not pay for 5,000 separate AI generations.

We should begin with a **small, curated collection of AI/ML papers**, rather than importing everything. PDF/figure reuse will depend on each paper's license.

## 5. Proposed Free vs Premium Plan

These are **example limits** to test against real usage and API costs, not final commitments.

| Feature | Free Tier | Premium Tier |
|---|---|---|
| Discover and search papers | Unlimited browsing | Unlimited browsing |
| Read Paper Cards (Bangla/English) | Unlimited | Unlimited |
| Figure-First Reader | Unlimited for available figures | Unlimited for available figures |
| Three-Step Reading Tracker | Unlimited | Unlimited |
| Prerequisite lessons via Learn | Free core lessons | Free core lessons + optional advanced learning content |
| Related Papers & Reading Paths | Unlimited | Unlimited |
| Reviews & Discussions | Read and participate | Read and participate |
| Paper Clubs (future) | Join available public clubs | Join public clubs + optional advanced club tools |
| **Research Landscape Map (future)** | **1 starter map per month**, up to 8 papers | **4 expanded maps per month**, up to 25 papers each |
| **Paper Comparison Matrix (future)** | **1 comparison per month**, up to 3 papers | **8 comparisons per month**, up to 5 papers each |
| **Research Claim & Evidence Check (future)** | **1 check per month**, one claim from a supported paper | **10 checks per month**, one claim per check |
| **Reproduction Roadmap (future)** | **1 trial per account** for a supported paper | **3 roadmaps per month** for supported papers |

**Why these limits?** Ordinary reading uses prepared data and costs little per additional user. Advanced research workflows need new AI processing and sometimes multiple source fetches, so they have tighter quotas and strict per-run paper/page/token limits. The sample quantities above must be revised after testing actual costs; Premium is never unlimited.

Premium should be sold as **more convenience and AI assistance**, not as permission to access basic education.

## 6. How We Avoid Losing Money

1. **Generate once, reuse many times.** Prepare standard paper content in advance and store it.
2. **Limit costly requests.** Enforce monthly usage limits on the backend, including Premium accounts. No “unlimited AI” promise.
3. **Cap individual job size.** Set maximum papers, pages, source text, model tokens, and processing time. Avoid unrestricted user-uploaded PDF processing in the initial release.
4. **Monitor actual costs.** Measure average cost per AI request, active users, hosting cost, and total monthly expense before finalizing pricing.
5. **Start small.** Launch free research-reading features first; release paid AI features only when their measured costs and working subscription payments are understood.
6. **Protect the budget.** Use per-user quotas, abuse prevention, caching, and an overall monthly AI budget. If the budget is reached, pause optional AI requests rather than incur uncontrolled charges.

**Subscription pricing proposal:** Set a low, student-friendly monthly price **after a pilot**. It must cover payment fees, hosting contribution, and the **realistic maximum cost** of Premium AI allowances, with a safety margin. Do not choose a price based only on average usage. If the proposed included runs cost too much, **lower the quota or narrow the job scope before launching**.

**Practical cost safeguard:** Count each finished workflow as one job; refuse requests beyond the paper/page cap; cache identical results; use small models for extraction and stronger models only for difficult synthesis; set a per-user and platform-wide spend ceiling. A monthly quota alone is not sufficient when one job could read hundreds of pages.

## 7. Recommended Release Order

- **Initial Research release:** Paper discovery, Paper Cards, reading tracker, prerequisite links, related papers, available figures, and reviews/discussions.
- **Next release:** More reading experiences and Paper Clubs, depending on feedback.
- **Optional Premium release:** Research Landscape Maps, Paper Comparison Matrices, Claim & Evidence Checks, and Reproduction Roadmaps — with tested limits, saved results, source citations, and moderation.

## 8. Decisions for the Client

- Agree on the **initial Research feature list** and the five-tab navigation.
- Decide whether the first paper collection should focus only on **AI/ML** (recommended).
- Approve the **free-first, paid-for-extra-AI-usage** approach in principle.
- Review actual costs before approving **subscription pricing and final usage quotas**.

**Bottom line:** Doshomik Bytes remains a free learning platform. Premium adds **structured research work products**, not generic chatbot access. Quotas and pricing are proposals to validate against real operating costs.
